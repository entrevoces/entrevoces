[SERIES: La última palabra. Episode: La reunión que no había terminado. Scene 1 — A community room after a potluck. Glasses clink and someone insists on wrapping leftover arroz in a suspiciously tiny container.]

NARRATOR: Teresa wanted a quiet Thursday. She had paid her electricity bill, remembered her umbrella and turned off the neighborhood group chat. The universe viewed this as an invitation.

LUPE: Teresa, please tell me the difference between a hearing and a judgment, but do it before the casserole gets cold.

TERESA: One is an event where the court may hear something. The other is a decision. Why?

LUPE: Because the entire building believes the hallway has been sentenced to eviction.

MARTA: I never said the hallway was evicted! I said the notice scheduled a hearing. Someone forwarded a photo without the second page.

RAFA: I forwarded the photo because the second page was a picture of my thumb. My thumb is a public document now.

[Music: playful bass walks two notes and stops.]

NARRATOR: At the center of the table lay a page folded into the shape of a boat. Its printed words had crossed three phones, six people and seven opinions.

VIOLETA: Before anyone purchases a moving box, we need to know what the document actually says. I can point to the words, but I am not your lawyer.

MARTA: Then let's read it slowly.

TERESA: The phrase says a hearing is scheduled for Tuesday. It does not say the judge has made a ruling. If there are questions about the case, we need a legal professional, not a group chat verdict.

LUPE: That is disappointing. A group chat verdict is much cheaper.

[Scene 2 — Teresa's kitchen, late evening. Rain at the windows. She practices consecutive interpreting with recordings from a training course.]

ACTOR ON RECORDING (English): The neighbor told me a red truck was parked outside. I did not see who drove it.

TRAINEE RECORDING (Spanish): Vi al acusado estacionar una camioneta roja afuera.

TERESA: No, no, no. Who saw the driver? No one. Who said the truck belonged to the defendant? No one. Where did that certainty come from?

RAFA: It came from a person who wanted the story to have a villain. Sometimes a red truck is just a red truck.

TERESA: You are very wise for someone who thought “every other day” meant twice a day.

RAFA: I have grown since Tuesday. The calendar and I have agreed to see other people.

[Pause. The group chat starts vibrating again.]

LUPE (on voice note): Emergency. Violeta has located the original notice, and the second page is not Rafa's thumb. It lists an apartment number.

TERESA: Which number?

LUPE: That's the strange part. It is not Marta's.

[Scene 3 — Apartment hallway. Shoes shuffle. A second envelope is slipped under a door. Nobody sees who delivered it.]

MARTA: I panicked, and I had everyone else panicking. I heard “court” and decided I knew the end of the story.

VIOLETA: The court documents may be serious. Don't replace careful reading with reassurance either. We can acknowledge uncertainty without inventing a happy outcome.

TERESA: That's the part I keep practicing. An interpreter isn't a narrator who fixes the story. We keep the story faithful to the speaker.

LUPE: But you can still be a good friend after the interpreting is over.

TERESA: Yes. Listening and helping are different jobs, and I need to know which one I am doing.

[Scene 4 — Door at the end of the hall. Someone opens it a few inches.]

UNKNOWN WOMAN: Are you all discussing the paper for unit seven?

MARTA: Do you know anything?

UNKNOWN WOMAN: I know the person listed has been away for six months. And I know the envelope was delivered today. I don't know whether anyone has been notified before.

LUPE: Finally! A person who knows what she knows and doesn't know what she doesn't know. Hire her for the group chat.

[Cliffhanger: the woman unfolds a second notice. The camera would show only her expression; the audio leaves the paper silent.]

NARRATOR: In the building where everyone translated everyone, the most important sentence might be the one no one had heard yet.

[End music, not a factual claim about any real eviction. NEXT EPISODE: Before the Signature.]
