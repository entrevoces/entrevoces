[Hallway of an apartment building, evening. Keys jingle. Neighbors overlap, but their voices are always intelligible.]

MARTA: The paper says they scheduled a hearing for next Tuesday. I don't know what the judge will decide.

INTERPRETER (deliberately altered training version): El juez decidió que tenemos que irnos el martes.

[Pause; a notification chimes, then three more. The disagreement is supposed to sound small.]

LUPE: Wait—who decided we have to leave? The paper says something about going to court, not moving trucks.

TERESA: In this simulation, the English speaker described a future hearing and uncertainty. The Spanish version changed that into a completed judicial decision.

MARTA: My cousin already borrowed a truck. I was going to pack the refrigerator before dinner.

VIOLETA: A scheduled hearing is not a final judgment. I am not deciding your case; I am saying what that notice actually says. If you need legal guidance, speak with a qualified source.

LUPE: So the refrigerator is not on trial?

VIOLETA: Not unless it files a motion.

[The practice version replays, this time with the correct line and a beat of silence after “no sé.”]

INTERPRETER (corrected training version): El documento dice que programaron una audiencia para el próximo martes. No sé qué decidirá el juez.

[The actors repeat the original in a new order, one speaker stopping mid-sentence for a repair. The learner listens for tense, attribution and certainty instead of isolated nouns.]

MARTA: I was scared because I thought a hearing meant the case was over.

LUPE: You were scared because the words arrived wearing the wrong uniform.

TERESA: And the interpreter's job is not to dress the words up. It's to carry what was actually said.

[End: the apartment group chat buzzes, but nobody has sent the refrigerator emoji yet.]
