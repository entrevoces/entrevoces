[COLD OPEN. A bus kneels at a curb. A zipper catches. At the end of an old corridor, a latch taps twice in the wind.]

NORA: On the radio you can see everything. That's the first lie I told myself when I took this job.

JAIME: (over intercom) The first lie was that you knew how to operate the espresso machine.

NORA: I know how to operate it. I refuse to negotiate with its values.

[THEME: three notes from a muted trumpet. A telephone line clicks.]

NORA: Good evening, neighbors. This is Radio La Cosa Exacta, where we ask what happened before announcing what we wish had happened. Tonight: somebody has sent us a picture of an apartment door, and somehow the whole building believes it proves a crime. Our producer Jaime has put the photograph in an envelope so I can't embellish it with my eyebrows.

JAIME: Your eyebrows have never accepted a chain of custody.

NORA: We are not a courtroom, Jaime. That is exactly why we must be careful. First call—Antonia, you're on.

ANTONIA: Mi vecina me dijo que vio la puerta abierta a las diez. That's what I said. But the neighborhood message says I saw a stranger forcing it.

NORA: What did you actually see?

ANTONIA: La foto. On my niece's phone. The door was closed in the photo, anyway. My neighbor said she saw it open earlier. I was buying bread.

JAIME: For the record, the bread was innocent.

ANTONIA: No lo sé. It was very expensive.

NORA: We have three separate things: what the neighbor reported, what Antonia saw in the picture, and what someone imagined between them. Which one of those does the chat remember?

ANTONIA: The imagined one. It has better punctuation.

[The studio door opens. A quiet woman in a raincoat enters without making eye contact with the microphone.]

MERCEDES: I'm the building's evening caretaker. I heard you're describing our door.

NORA: You can choose not to come on the air.

MERCEDES: I know. But I want to tell my side before the door acquires an attorney. I was closing the upper window when the wind pushed the latch. The door was open for perhaps twenty seconds. Nobody was forcing it.

NORA: Are you certain about the twenty seconds?

MERCEDES: Approximately. I did not time it. The radio at the bakery was playing the chorus of something terrible.

JAIME: Please don't tell me it was our morning show.

MERCEDES: It was. The chorus was the weather forecast. Twice.

[A second telephone line flashes. The tiny light in the booth looks like an impatient firefly.]

NORA: We also have an interpretation exercise, recorded by actors, that shows how a rumor can escape its owner. Let's hear the original witness account first.

WITNESS ACTOR (ENGLISH): My neighbor told me that the door was open. I didn't see anyone enter.

INTERPRETER ACTOR (SPANISH): Mi vecina me dijo que la puerta estaba abierta. No vi entrar a nadie.

NORA: That was the faithful version. Now listen to the fictional mistake.

INTERPRETER ACTOR (SPANISH, SECOND TAKE): Vi a una persona entrar por la puerta abierta.

[A bell sounds once, dry as a teaspoon striking a glass.]

ANTONIA: ¡No! That's a completely different witness.

JAIME: In one sentence we've created a spectator, an entrant, and a certainty that weren't there.

NORA: Exactly. The problem isn't the accent. It isn't whether the Spanish is elegant. It's that the speaker's limits were stolen. Can we repair it without turning the whole segment into a sermon?

MERCEDES: Play the original again. The people listening can do the work.

[They do. This time the silence after the sentence is long enough to hear rain touch the studio window.]

NORA: Mercedes, what do you want the neighbors to know?

MERCEDES: That somebody should repair the latch, and somebody else should apologize to the woman they accused. I have more important gossip: the community garden has grown three tomatoes in October.

ANTONIA: Three? They said there were nine.

JAIME: Here we go again.

NORA: (softening) We may have solved the door. We have not solved the internet. At least the tomatoes are still negotiable.

[The photograph is finally removed from its envelope. It's a close-up of the latch, taken a week before the rumor. A penciled note on the back says, “Ask maintenance.”]

NORA: That is our final fact for tonight: a photo can show a latch; it cannot show what somebody witnessed on another day.

[THEME RETURNS, interrupted by Jaime's espresso machine grinding with the determination of a small construction vehicle.]

JAIME: I got it to make coffee.

NORA: Is it coffee?

JAIME: I wouldn't testify to that.

[END. The call-in line remains open for listeners who want to hear the two versions again.]
