## Chapter 2 · Who May Interpret: Qualified Bilingual Staff and the Narrow Emergency Exception

# Who May Interpret: Qualified Bilingual Staff and the Narrow Emergency Exception

## The work problem

A bilingual staff member is available, a patient needs language support, and the schedule is moving. The practical question is not, "Does this employee speak another language?" It is, "Does this employee meet the source's test for a *qualified* bilingual/multilingual staff member, and if not, does the narrow emergency exception apply?"

The source answers the first part directly: "Section 1557 states that this is permissible, but the bilingual/multilingual staff member must qualified." [p1-0] Permissibility is therefore conditional. Bilingualism opens the question; it does not settle it.

## The conceptual model: a two-part qualified test

Under the source, *qualified* has two cumulative parts. Both must be present.

**1. Proficiency in both languages, including specialized language.** The staff member must be: "Proficient in speaking and understanding both spoken English and at least one other spoken language or sign language, including any necessary specialized vocabulary, terminology and phraseology" [p1-0]. Note the scope: speaking *and* understanding, English *and* the other spoken language or sign language, and not only everyday fluency — the proficiency must include any necessary specialized vocabulary, terminology and phraseology.

**2. Effective, accurate, impartial direct communication.** The staff member must also be: "Able to effectively, accurately, and impartially communicate directly with individuals who use sign language or who have limited English proficiency." [p1-0] This is a performance criterion, not a language-knowledge criterion alone. It adds three qualities — effectively, accurately, and impartially — and it requires direct communication with the individual concerned.

If either part is missing, the staff member does not meet these criteria. That conclusion then triggers the emergency rule below; it does not create a middle category of "qualified enough for routine work."

## Terms in context

| Term as used here | What it means in this chapter |
|---|---|
| **bilingual/multilingual staff member** | A staff member who uses more than one language. The label alone does not establish qualification. |
| **qualified bilingual/multilingual staff** | A bilingual/multilingual staff member who meets both parts above: proficiency including specialized vocabulary, terminology and phraseology, and effective, accurate, impartial direct communication. [p1-0] |
| **limited English proficiency** | The population named in the second criterion, alongside individuals who use sign language, with whom the staff member must be able to communicate directly. [p1-0] |
| **emergency involving imminent threat** | The only situation in which staff who do not meet the criteria may provide interpretation services, framed by the source as a threat "to the safety or welfare of an individual or the public." [p1-0] |

## Decision workflow — inferred teaching design

The sequence below is a teaching aid for applying the sourced criteria; steps that go beyond restating those criteria are labeled inferred.

1. **Check proficiency against the sourced criterion.** Is the person proficient in speaking and understanding both spoken English and at least one other spoken language or sign language, including any necessary specialized vocabulary, terminology and phraseology? [p1-0]
2. **Check direct communication against the sourced criterion.** Can the person effectively, accurately, and impartially communicate directly with individuals who use sign language or who have limited English proficiency? [p1-0]
