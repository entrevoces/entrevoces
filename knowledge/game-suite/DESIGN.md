# The interpreting collection — direct authoring contract

The user explicitly authorized direct design and implementation in this chat after the local CLI exhausted its account quota. These are real local experiments. Independent CLI reviews remain pending; no generic self-review is represented as four independent executions. Nothing is automatically published.

Interpreting is the shared responsibility: notice load-bearing meaning, retain it, reformulate it, coordinate under load, and carry it between people. The games reuse existing medical/legal terms and interpreting-skill knowledge. Synthetic world instructions are practice designs, never clinical advice or universal legal rules. English–Spanish and English–Chinese are selectable. A game only records the limited responsibility its actual action assessed; movement, score, opening a page and winning by motor skill are not language proof.

## Candidate space and selection

A conversation tree alone, a glossary card battler and a courtroom document desk were rejected: they would return to text dialogs, answer selection and overlapping recall. A pure rhythm game was rejected because pressing to beeps does not demonstrate interpreting or listening. A walking simulator was rejected because travel without consequential language decisions would be decorative.

The selected collection uses five different state spaces: an optical logic network, an explorable memory/logistics map, a concurrent channel simulation, a kinetic platform world, and a turn-based encounter city. They share meaning primitives and a learning engine, not the same activity dressed five ways. There is no claim of globally unprecedented mechanics; the work is original expression built from broad systemic game conventions, without copied characters, art, audio or distinctive commercial layouts.

1. **Lumen Circuit — REFRACT.** Untimed optical construction. Rotate physical conduits to carry a signal through negation, uncertainty and quantity transformations. Wrong routes change the represented meaning or dissipate the beam. Teaches attention to load-bearing qualifiers through a spatial system.
2. **Drift Archive — NAVIGATE.** Explore floating islands while retaining a multi-part message. Plan a route, remember order and forbidden locations, and use limited personal markers. Wind and changing connections make navigation a strategy, not a multiple-choice answer. Builds on qualifier attention by adding memory and sequencing.
3. **Counterpoint — CONDUCT.** Control concurrent speaker channels and translation receivers. Buffer complete units, avoid clipping a turn, route the correct target-language term and decide when clarification is worth the time. Builds memory into divided attention and timed bilingual retrieval. Musical cues are feedback, not claimed spoken-source assessment.
4. **Marginfall — TRAVERSE.** A kinetic platform world whose brief changes the traversable route. Jump, air-dash, read intent ahead of movement and preserve conditional/negative instructions while the world folds. Builds language attention into forward planning; motor falls do not count as failed language understanding.
5. **Common Ground — COORDINATE.** A turn-based living city of speaker intentions. Choose which encounter to serve, carry bounded meaning, seek clarification rather than inventing missing information, and deliver to the intended participant before plans collide. Integrates prior attention, memory and coordination in a different tactical decision system.

## Shared production contract

Each game starts on its own full-screen title scene with one Play/Continue action. During play at least 80% of the viewport is the canvas world. Source context is a short utterance, not a dialog wall. HUD shows only chapter/round, immediate state and essential tools. Definitions, source notes, full controls and design prose are optional help. No game is a quiz dialog, flashcard deck, card sorter or text desk.

Simulation runs at 60 Hz through the shared GameLoop, with bounded catch-up and no background time. SceneMachine owns title, play, pause, interlude and result. Input owns tap/keyboard state and releases on blur. Camera maps CSS-pixel input; every world lays out from the actual viewport rather than shrinking a desktop board. Targets are at least 44 CSS pixels where possible; taps have keyboard equivalents. Canvas DPR is capped at 2. Renderer and simulation are separate; deterministic RNG owns generation. AssetManager loads local title art and cue bytes. AudioMixer unlocks after a gesture, handles mute/volume and keeps play available when audio fails. VersionedStore owns settings and level checkpoints, with corrupt/quota recovery. EngineClient owns validated, append-only, domain-isolated evidence. Shared engine-core owns mastery, retention, assessment and difficulty.

Checkpoints preserve level and seed, not an invented exact-frame resume. Blur opens pause. Reload returns to the current level checkpoint; it never simulates elapsed time. Hint use remains assisted for the current challenge after a reload. Restart does not silently grant progress. Every assessed attempt has an event ID; a completed attempt cannot award twice. Game score and movement never become mastery by themselves. Real-time difficulty derives from the deterministic policy; comfort mode removes pressure without pretending assisted play was independent proof.

All titles have finite sessions, meaningful escalation, a win/result, recoverable failure, replay with a new seed, pause, restart, help, mute, language choice, reduced motion and checkpoint continuation. A complete session must be played and inspected before any release claim. Adversarial checks include loops/soft locks, random-input success, waiting/speed shortcuts, repeated evidence, repeated restart, phone portrait, keyboard operation, blur and malformed storage. The native independent design/code review gates remain pending because of the account quota; local implementation and observed tests will be reported separately.
