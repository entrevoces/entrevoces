Build a substantial practitioner handbook for legal interpreters. Do not center Oregon or a single US state, and do not pretend that one jurisdiction's legal rules apply worldwide. Distinguish portable interpreting skills and conceptual vocabulary from jurisdiction-specific institutions, offenses, procedures and rights. Compare common-law/civil-law and institutional differences only where credible primary evidence supports the comparison. Explicitly label country/court/procedure boundaries.

The research agent must derive an extensive topical professional coverage plan and acquire real authoritative court, judiciary, legal-aid/public legal education and professional interpreter sources. Cover court and hearing roles, legal language/register, parties and capacity, criminal/civil/administrative pathways, allegations/charges/claims, arrest/custody/release, hearings/trial/evidence/testimony, examination and objections, findings/verdict/judgment/sentence/order, remedy/enforcement/appeal, relevant family/immigration/employment/housing contexts where justified, rights and procedural fairness, interpreter ethics/role/conflicts/confidentiality, managing communication without giving advice, witness speech/disfluency/profanity/register, numbers/dates/names, ambiguity and requests to clarify. These are coverage responsibilities to evaluate and refine, not permission to invent rules or imply encyclopedic coverage.

Explain the conceptual models and the work problem before important terminology. Include consequential distinctions and non-equivalent institutions, bilingual pitfalls/false friends, terminology in procedural sequences, labeled synthetic situations and source-linked definition supplements. Avoid word-for-word equivalence tables with no legal context. Source specific legal statements and their jurisdiction. No personalized legal advice. Demonstrate source grounding topic by topic, discover additional sources for weaknesses, and disclose currentness/rights/language limits. The final handbook should be cohesive, extensive where the actual practice requires it, and useful as an interpreter's professional reference.
